Claimant-Side Tool Evaluator

Module A, the Due Process Calibrator, fixes the level of countervailing capacity the state owes when it deploys an automated decision system. This module asks the next question, the one in steps 3 and 4 of the framework's procedure: does the claimant-side tool that actually exists deliver that level? It scores thirteen capabilities on evidence, rolls them up into the framework's five proportionality dimensions, and measures the due-process gap between what is required and what is delivered, read against the duty itself.

This is a design instrument, not a legal finding. It evaluates the claimant-side tool, never the government system, and never intentions or marketing claims: a capability exists only if evidence shows it, and where evidence is insufficient the lower score applies. The percentages and level anchors are study choices with no legal authority. Evidence rule · CC Deployment framework · MIT License

Step 1

The tool under evaluation

Describe the claimant-side tool as it exists, who ships it and what it claims, then set the level Module A assigned to the government system it counters. The required level is set by hand, exactly as the calibrator produced it.

Step 2

Thirteen capabilities, on evidence

Score only what you can show. Every capability starts at zero with no evidence, because under the framework a feature that cannot be demonstrated does not exist. The evidence setting caps the score: verified in use reaches the full scale, documented-only is capped at 3, and no evidence counts as 0 and is reported as unverified. The tag under each capability names its anchor in the thesis and the proportionality dimension it feeds.

How the numbers are built

Each capability's effective score is the slider value limited by its evidence cap. Delivered capacity is the plain average of the thirteen effective scores, expressed as a percentage of the full scale. The required level maps to a required percentage, Minimal 30, Moderate 55, High 80, Maximal 92, and the due-process gap is the shortfall in points between the two. The gap is then read relative to the duty itself, gap divided by the required percentage, so delivering nothing against a Minimal duty is a 100% shortfall of that duty, not a small absolute number. The relative shortfall sets the five bands, under 20 minimal deficit, under 40 moderate, under 60 serious, under 80 critical, and 80 or above structural failure, and the compliance reading, no gap fully compliant, under 40 partially, otherwise non-compliant. One exception outranks the average: when any dimension sits at or near its floor, the verdict is withheld from full compliance even at zero gap, because under the framework the lowest dimension is the binding constraint and floors are minimums an average cannot buy back. Because the capabilities are averaged, the dimensions carry unequal implicit weight, Technical five capabilities of thirteen, Accountability three, Economic and Empirical two each, Temporal one, a study choice, disclosed as such. All of these numbers are study choices with no legal authority.

            Manual

            How to use the evaluator

            1. What this tool is, and is not

            The Countervailing Co-Deployment framework runs in two modules. Module A, the Due Process Calibrator, classifies the government's automated system and fixes the co-deployment level the state owes, steps 1 and 2 of the framework's procedure. This module is steps 3 and 4: it scores an already existing claimant-side counterpart on the framework's terms and applies the proportionality dimensions to it. The question is never whether the tool is good in the abstract. The question is whether the tool is sufficient for the due-process obligation the automated system generates.

            It evaluates the claimant-side tool, not the government system, and it evaluates capabilities actually available to the claimant, not intentions or marketing claims.

            2. The evidence discipline

            Every capability carries an evidence setting, and the setting caps the score. Verified in use opens the full 0 to 5 scale. Documented only, claims in manuals or announcements that were not observed working, is capped at 3, no undemonstrated feature can score above the midpoint. No evidence counts as 0, the framework assumes a capability exists only if there is concrete evidence that it exists, and every capability left in that state is reported, by name, as unverified rather than merely absent. When in doubt between two settings, choose the lower one.

            3. The thirteen capabilities and where they come from

            Capabilities 1 to 5 are the five claimant functions of thesis chapter 4, translation, eligibility explanation, evidence mapping, error and mismatch detection, and contestation support, they feed the Technical dimension. Accessibility and cost of contestation feed the Economic dimension, parity of effort and deployer-funded contestation. Continuous maintenance feeds the Temporal dimension. Resistance to category mimicry, a chapter 9 design condition, and practical effectiveness feed the Empirical dimension. Explainability of the tool itself and claimant data sovereignty, both chapter 9 design conditions, and human escalation feed the Accountability dimension. The five dimensions and their floors are chapter 6's, quoted unchanged from Module A. Because the thirteen capabilities are averaged with equal weight, the dimensions weigh unequally in the delivered score, Technical the most at five of thirteen, Temporal the least at one, a study choice, stated here so no one mistakes it for a claim of the thesis.

            4. Reading the result

            Press Evaluate the tool to produce the result, the button asks you to choose the required level first if you have not. If you change any input afterwards, the flag Inputs changed, evaluate again stays up until you re-evaluate, and everything on screen, including the Word report, keeps following the last evaluated result, never the moved controls.

            The gauge shows the due-process gap read against the duty itself, the shortfall as a share of what the assigned level requires, in five bands. Fully compliant means no gap and no dimension at its floor. Compliant on average, a floor is breached, means the averages meet the level but at least one dimension sits at or near its floor, the framework withholds full compliance there because the lowest dimension is the binding constraint, and the final qualitative floor judgment rests with you. Partially compliant covers relative shortfalls under 40, non-compliant the rest. The levels line reconciles the two readings of the same result, the band and the level ladder, by stating how many points short of the required level the tool sits. The criterion shown with each dimension is its top anchor, what a 5 looks like, not a description of the observed state, the floor beneath it is the minimum. The weakest points rank the three lowest dimensions, quoting the floor only where a dimension actually sits at or near it. The smallest set of improvements lists, in order of impact, the capability gains that would close the gap, starting with what is unverified, and its last item stops at the exact point that closes it. Saved evaluations live in this browser's localStorage, use Export as JSON to take them to another machine and Import JSON to bring them back, imports merge by id and never overwrite what is already here.

            5. Saving your work, and the Word report

            Save this evaluation stores the current tool, its evidence record, and the result in this browser on this device. It never leaves the machine. Load reopens a saved evaluation and re-evaluates it, Delete removes one, and Clear all empties the list, Export as JSON and Import JSON move the whole list to and from a file, for backup or for a second device.

            Once you have evaluated a tool, Download the Word report writes a .docx of the whole case, the tool description and the required level, every capability's score with its evidence setting and note, the five-dimension roll-up, the weakest points, the smallest set of improvements, the unverified list, and the final written opinion. The file is assembled entirely inside the browser, offline, nothing is sent anywhere, the same machinery Module A uses for its own report.
