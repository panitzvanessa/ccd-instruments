How the heuristic works

The duty intensity starts from the average of severity, vulnerability and error risk, is reduced as the agency burden grows, rises with the asymmetry profile, and rises again when contested KDMAs are present. The intensity then maps onto the framework's required co-deployment level, 0 to 3, with the maximal band reserved for opaque automation, immediate severe harm, a vulnerable and often offline population, and no human point of recourse. Directions come from the framework and the case law. The exact weights are study choices with no legal authority, which is why the meter always ships the written analysis alongside the number.

        Manual

        How to use the calibrator

        A complete guide, what the meter is for, how to run it step by step, how to read the result, a worked example, and the sources behind every factor.

        1. What this tool is, and is not

        The calibrator is the due process meter of the Countervailing Co-Deployment framework. You feed it one government-run automated system, described bare, before any safeguards, and it returns two things, the intensity of the due-process duty the state owes the affected person, and the co-deployment level, 0 to 3, that the claimant-side countervailing tool must reach to meet that duty.

        It is not a court, and not a predictor. The meter does not estimate whether a lawsuit would win or lose. It sizes an obligation and the counterpart that obligation requires. The number is a study heuristic whose weights carry no legal authority, which is why it always travels with the written analysis.

        The engine runs two aligned registers at once. One is the framework's classification step, the magnitude of the asymmetry the system creates, the severity and reversibility of the harm, and the vulnerability of the affected population. The other is the three-factor balance of Mathews v. Eldridge. As the thesis puts it, a reader who wants to know how much co-deployment is owed can run the Mathews balance and the proportionality profile together, because they ask the same question in two registers.

        Of the five-step procedure in chapter 6, the calibrator runs steps 1 and 2, classify the system and set the required co-deployment level, and then drafts the countervailing package that level demands. Steps 3 and 4, scoring an already deployed counterpart on the five dimensions and applying their floors as the binding constraint, are the job of Module B, the Claimant-Side Tool Evaluator, a companion single file built on the same framework, and the five-axes card at the end of the results here is the hand reference for the same exercise.

        2. Opening and saving

        • Open. The tool is a single offline file. Save it and double click, or drag it into a browser tab. No internet, no install, nothing to configure.
        • Saving your work. The Save button stores an analysis in this browser on this device, through localStorage. It never leaves the machine. Load reopens a saved analysis and recalibrates it, Delete removes one, and Clear all empties the list.
        • The Word report. After calibrating, Download the Word report writes a .docx of the whole case, the system description, every input value, the written analysis, the countervailing package, and the five-axes checklist. The file is assembled entirely inside the browser, offline, nothing is sent anywhere.
        • A note on viewers. If you open the file inside a viewer that blocks storage, the tool still runs, it simply tells you that saving is unavailable.
        • Links from companion tools. A sibling file, like SNAP Navigator, can open this calibrator with the inputs pre-filled through the link itself. The data travels inside the link on your device, nothing is fetched and nothing is sent, and a malformed link simply loads the page normally.

        3. Quick start in six moves

        1. Load or describe a system. Tap a profile chip for a worked starting point, or type your own system into the box in Step 1.
        2. Answer the guided questions. Step 1.5 walks all twelve inputs in plain language, and every answer moves its slider in real time. Skip it with Fill in manually if you prefer to work the sliders directly.
        3. Classify the stakes. In Step 2, set severity and reversibility of harm, vulnerability of the population, and risk of error. Set the deployer's burden, which pushes the other way.
        4. Rate the asymmetry. In Step 3, rate the six characteristics of the bare system, opacity, contestability, human recourse, proprietary secrecy, validation, and decisiveness.
        5. Flag contested value judgments. In Step 4, set the KDMA input and name the values in tension, if any.
        6. Calibrate and read. Press Calibrate the duty. The meter shows the required level, the written analysis, the countervailing package, and the five axes on which to judge the package once you build it, and Download the Word report exports it all as a .docx.

        4. The controls, one by one

        Step 1, the system

        Describe what the system decides, about whom, and what the person stands to lose. The six profile chips load bare-system study suggestions drawn from the kinds of systems the case law and the thesis have met, benefit eligibility screening, fraud adjudication, a Medicaid care-hours formula, public-worker scoring, a sentencing risk score, and appointment gatekeeping. A profile fills the description, the sliders, and the KDMA input, so you can calibrate at once or adjust first.

        Step 1.5, the guided assessment

        Twelve objective questions, one for each control the meter reads, eleven selectors and a text field for the values in tension. Every answer moves its slider the moment you pick it, and the tag under each question names the control it drives. The link runs both ways, moving a slider, loading a profile, or reopening a saved analysis updates the matching answers, so the two views never disagree. A question left unanswered leaves its slider exactly where it is, the meter never fills gaps for you. Fill in manually collapses the questions if you prefer the sliders directly.

        Step 2, classify the stakes

        Four sliders, each from 1 to 5. Three raise the duty, one lowers it.

        Severity and reversibility of harm. What the person loses, and whether it can be undone. This is the private interest in the Mathews register. Goldberg v. Kelly, 397 U.S. 254 (1970); Mathews v. Eldridge, 424 U.S. 319 (1976)
        Vulnerability of the affected population. How equipped the people on the receiving end are to understand and respond. CC Deployment thesis, classification input
        Risk of error and value of safeguards. How likely the bare system is to get it wrong, and how much a counterpart would help. The second Mathews factor. K.W. v. Armstrong (D. Idaho 2016)
        The deployer's burden. The cost stronger safeguards would impose on the deploying party. This is the counterweight, the third Mathews factor, shown in red because a heavy burden pulls the duty down. Mathews v. Eldridge, 424 U.S. 319 (1976)

        Step 3, the asymmetry profile

        Six sliders describing the bare system, each from 1 to 5, where 5 is the most concerning. Rate the system as it runs before any countervailing tool, because the result tells you what that tool must add. The six are opacity, whether the person can get the actual reasons, contestability, whether the specific output can be verified and challenged, human recourse, whether a person sits in the loop, proprietary secrecy, whether the logic is shielded as a trade secret, validation, whether the system was tested on this population with reliable data, and decisiveness, whether the output effectively decides by itself.

        Step 4, the KDMA input

        KDMA stands for key decision-making attributes, the value-laden traits that steer a decision where no single right answer exists. The term comes from the DARPA In the Moment line of research on decisions of exactly that kind, and it is an extension this calibrator adds beyond the dissertation, whose chapters do not use the term. The selector has three settings, no contested KDMAs, contested at the margins, and a core value trade-off. The text field lets you name the values in tension, for example fraud prevention against access to benefits.

        Why the KDMA input matters. When the system trades one contested value against another, the framework treats the decision as one where reasonable people disagree, so four safeguards become mandatory in the package, individualized explanations, a route to submit additional evidence, mandatory human review, and an appeal process.

        5. Reading the result

        Calibrate produces four blocks. The gauge places the system on a spectrum of duty intensity and names the required co-deployment level. The written analysis walks each input in turn, in plain language, and cites the case or framework chapter behind it. The countervailing package is the list of safeguards the tool must deliver, each traced to a source. The five-axes card is the checklist for judging that package once it exists. Below them, Download the Word report exports the whole result, inputs, analysis, package, and checklist, as a .docx assembled offline in the browser.

        If you change any input after a result, the meter raises the flag Inputs changed, calibrate again until you recalibrate. Everything on screen, and the Word report, keeps following the last calibrated result, never the moved sliders, so a report can never mix two states of the inputs.

        The four levels map duty intensity onto what the framework requires.

        LevelWhen it triggersWhat the counterpart must deliver
        Level 0 to 1, minimalSmall asymmetry, low and reversible stakes, a population that can fend for itself.Static information and reasoned notice.
        Level 2, moderateReal asymmetry and moderate harm, decisions that shape access but rarely subsistence.An interactive counterpart that helps the person understand eligibility, map evidence, and detect mismatch before rejection.
        Level 3, highLarge asymmetry, severe or hard-to-reverse harm, a vulnerable population, decisions that touch subsistence or liberty.A corrective counterpart connected to usable routes for appeal, human review, evidence supplementation, and rule revision.
        Level 3+, maximalOpaque automation, immediate severe harm, a poor and often offline population, and no human point of recourse.The level 3 counterpart, plus enforced maintenance and independent monitoring, it co-evolves with the system and an independent body answers for it.

        Levels are earned by realized capacity, not declared. As the thesis insists, a portal that exists but is unusable does not qualify as level 2 or 3, and an information leaflet no claimant can translate into action does not satisfy the principle.

        How the number is built

        The duty intensity starts from the average of severity, vulnerability, and error risk. It is reduced as the deployer's burden grows, raised as the asymmetry profile grows, and raised again when contested KDMAs are present. The result is clamped to the 1 to 5 scale and mapped onto the four levels, with the maximal band gated behind the framework's own trigger profile, so a system reaches it only with high intensity together with severe and hard-to-reverse harm, no human recourse, a vulnerable population, and opacity or proprietary secrecy. Band cutoffs are applied to the score as displayed, rounded to one decimal, so a raw value from 4.25 upward reads 4.3 and sits at the maximal cutoff. The weights are study choices with no legal authority. They order and structure the reasoning, they do not measure a legal quantity, which is why the analysis ships beside the number.

        6. A worked example, step by step

        Take the automated fraud system the thesis studies, the one whose facts echo the Michigan MiDAS episode. Follow the meter through it.

        Step 1 · Load the system

        Tap the Fraud adjudication chip. The box fills with a state system that flags claims as fraudulent and imposes penalties on its own, seizing refunds and garnishing wages, sending notice to a dormant online account. The guided answers of Step 1.5 fill themselves too, a profile keeps the questions and the sliders in sync.

        Step 2 · Classify the stakes

        The profile sets the harm high and hard to reverse, seized wages and lost subsistence, the population vulnerable, poor and often offline, and the error risk severe, this is the system a state audit later found wrong in the vast majority of reviewed cases. The deployer's burden is left low, since a notice that actually explains costs little.

        Step 3 · Rate the asymmetry

        Opacity and contestability sit near the top, the person cannot get real reasons or challenge the finding. Human recourse is at the maximum concern, the system auto-adjudicates. Validation is poor and decisiveness is near total.

        Step 4 · KDMA

        The profile marks a core value trade-off and names it, fraud prevention against access to benefits. This is the contested value judgment that makes the four safeguards mandatory.

        Step 5 · Calibrate and read

        The meter returns Level 3+, maximal duty. The analysis explains why, high and irreversible stakes on a vulnerable population, an opaque system that decides by itself with no human recourse, and a contested KDMA. The package then requires the full set, individualized notice, an evidence route, mandatory human review, an appeal, plus enforced maintenance and independent monitoring, because a body independent of the deployer must answer for the counterpart. Read the five-axes card last, it is how you will later check whether the counterpart you build actually clears each floor.

        Now change one input and watch the duty move. Drop human recourse to robust review and the system leaves the maximal band, because a human point of recourse is one of its triggers. Drop the harm to Significant and it leaves the band too, immediate and severe harm is part of the same trigger profile. Turn the KDMA setting to none and the four value-judgment safeguards recede. That sensitivity is the point, the meter shows how each design choice changes what the state owes.

        7. Sources behind the factors

        Every factor and safeguard traces to one of two places, the framework of the CC Deployment thesis, or a verified court decision. The cases below were checked against court opinions, official reporters, and legal summaries. Two provenances should be kept distinct. Goldberg, Mathews, and K.W. v. Armstrong are cited in the dissertation itself. Perdue, Houston Federation of Teachers, Bauserman, and Loomis were verified for this tool and extend the dissertation's citation record, which reaches the Michigan and Idaho episodes without naming these decisions.

        Legal anchors

        • Goldberg v. Kelly, 397 U.S. 254 (1970). Notice and a prior hearing before benefits are cut.
        • Mathews v. Eldridge, 424 U.S. 319 (1976). The three-factor balance the meter runs.
        • Perdue v. Gargano, 964 N.E.2d 825 (Ind. 2012). Vague automated denial notices violate due process.
        • K.W. v. Armstrong (9th Cir. 2015; D. Idaho 2016). A secret Medicaid formula disclosed, then struck as arbitrary on unreliable data.
        • Houston Federation of Teachers v. HISD, 251 F. Supp. 3d 1168 (S.D. Tex. 2017). Black-box scores the person cannot verify or challenge.
        • Bauserman v. Unemployment Ins. Agency, 983 N.W.2d 855 (Mich. 2022). Auto-adjudicated fraud and the harm of no human recourse.
        • State v. Loomis, 881 N.W.2d 749 (Wis. 2016), cert. denied, 137 S. Ct. 2290 (2017). A proprietary score may inform, but not determine, the outcome.

        Framework anchors, CC Deployment thesis

        • Chapter 4. The five claimant functions, translate, explain eligibility, map evidence, detect mismatch, route contestation.
        • Chapter 5. Why the duty is owed, and the mapping of the Mathews factors onto the framework's inputs.
        • Chapter 6. The classification step, the four duty-intensity bands, and the five proportionality dimensions with their floors.
        • Chapters 6 and 7. The maintenance obligation and independent monitoring the maximal band enforces.
        • Chapter 9. The four design conditions, capacity-building, contestability to the user, claimant data sovereignty, and resistance to category mimicry.
        • Chapter 10. Levels measured by realized capacity, not nominal provision.

        The KDMA input

        • Key decision-making attributes, from the DARPA In the Moment line of research on decisions where no single right answer exists. Used here as a manual framework input, not a claim about how any specific system computes its result. The concept does not appear in the dissertation's chapters, it is an extension adopted for this tool, and the four safeguards it makes mandatory are each independently anchored in the case law and framework chapters above.

        One standing caveat. The calibrator organizes reasoning under a framework. It does not deliver legal advice, and its numeric weights carry no legal authority. Treat the level as a structured argument about what the state owes, always read with the written analysis beside it.
